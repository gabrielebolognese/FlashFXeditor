## Global deviations
Decisions that diverge from docs/EDITOR_SPEC.md, with the reason. Each one also appears under its batch.
- The design system file is `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` (repo root), not `DESIGN_SYSTEMS.md` as the docs say. Every reference to `DESIGN_SYSTEMS.md` means this file. Reason: the real filename, kept as-is per NEXT_BATCH_SETUP §0.
- Styling uses plain CSS Modules + CSS custom properties, not the Tailwind named in `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`. Reason: spec §3.1 (binding) forbids Tailwind; DS values are kept verbatim as tokens. Approved with the Batch 01 plan. (Batch 01)
- Node engine is `>=22.12` (spec says nothing; scaffold said 20). Reason: Vitest 5 requires it. (Batch 01)
- Track heights live only in the document (`Track.height`, §5.2), not in session state as §5.3 also lists. Reason: one source of truth, and §5.2 types are required verbatim. (Batch 02)
- Undo restores the selection from before the undone command, redo the one after it (§6.19's wording is ambiguous). (Batch 02)
- Backups in `history/` are named with UTC timestamps (`project-YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS.json`, then `-2`, `-3`... for saves within one second). Reason: local time can run backwards at a daylight-saving fall-back, and the rotation (oldest name first) would then delete the new backups. (Batch 03)
- `edit.delete` (Delete/Backspace) is also bound in the `media` scope. Reason: §12 asks for Delete in the media panel while the §18 table lists only timeline and preview; §12 is the more specific rule. (Batch 04)
- Media items are created with their applicable analysis `pending` (§9.1); the IdleQueue consumer picks them up and queues placeholder jobs without writing the document (no undo steps). (Batch 04)
- The sample index is built from the moov's sample tables (read by `readMp4`, mdat never read), not by streaming the whole file through mp4box extraction (§9.2, Batch 06 Build). For the MP4/MOV files the app accepts, the moov already lists every sample's offset, size, dts, cts and sync flag; streaming would read gigabytes of mdat for nothing. Fragmented MP4 (samples in moof boxes) is refused by the probe since Batch 04. (Batch 06)
- Seeks feed the keyframe straight into the running decoder, with no `flush()` and no `reset()` (§9.2 says "flush, feed from the sync sample"). Measured in Chrome 155 on an RDNA2 GPU: 11 frames of 4K HEVC took ~110 ms fed straight on, and 300-600 ms after a flush or a reset + configure (1080p H.264: 32-55 ms against 137-152 ms). Frame requests feed only what their target needs (`feedUntil`), so the decoder is idle when the next seek comes. (Batch 06)
- Image clips may run past their media's `durationUs` (`validate.ts`): a still has `durationUs: 0` and §6.14 gives images 5 s. Video and audio clips stay bound by the media. Animated images (loop or hold) are Batch 34's. (Batch 07)
- Timeline zoom limits (§6.2 "fit-to-content down to 0.01 px/frame, up to 400") are applied only when the user zooms or scrolls, never on document changes, so an undo that shortens the content never moves the view. The zoom-out limit is Fit (content + 2 s); a view already below it is never pushed further out. (Batch 07)
- Session `timeline.scrollY` is relative to the resting divider position (the divider rests at half the track area height; video tracks stack up from it, audio tracks down, §6.2) and may be negative; session.json accepts negative values. (Batch 07)
- Commands carry a 10th invalidation flag, `composite` (§3.4 lists nine): the rendered frame changes but nothing the timeline draws does. `clip.update` patches that touch only `transform`, `opacity` or `crop` carry `composite` instead of `clips`, so the timeline never redraws for them (Batch 08 acceptance 4). (Batch 08)
- Default transform on insert is Fit for every media with a size, not only when the aspect differs: same-aspect media of another size fill the frame exactly (scale 1 when the sizes match). "1:1 scale otherwise" taken literally would show 4K phone footage at twice the 1080x1920 frame. (Batch 08)
- Media time 0 is the first frame: the compositor asks for `firstCts + sourceTime` (edit lists are still not applied, Batch 06), and picks the frame time nearest to it (§5.1). (Batch 08)
- The frame rate can change only while the project has no clips and no markers; resolution changes use "keep". Reason: the §15 conversions (fps ratio, "Scale layout") belong to Batch 42 (this batch's "Do not build yet"), and an unconverted fps change breaks the media-clip duration invariant. (Batch 03)
- Video frames are uploaded at most at the project's long edge, scaled on the GPU while uploading (§9.3 names no upload size). Reason: the preview never draws more of a frame at Fit or 100%, and 4K HEVC decodes faster scaled on upload (70 frames/s, against 56 copied at 4K). Zoom above 100% gets larger uploads in Batch 21. (Batch 09)
- MP4/MOV audio decodes with WebCodecs `AudioDecoder` in decode.worker, not `decodeAudioData` on the main thread (§14). Reason: `decodeAudioData` needs the whole file in memory (gigabytes for 4K phone video), cannot decode a range (so no 60 s segments past 10 min), and ignores the edit list (AAC priming: phone audio up to ~48 ms late). Other audio formats use `decodeAudioData`; past 10 minutes they still decode whole (Batch 45 follow-up). (Batch 10)
- The audio scheduler starts one `AudioBufferSourceNode` per clip (per 60 s segment and loop pass) at most 200 ms ahead, in 50 ms steps, playing to the clip's end, instead of a source per 50 ms chunk (§8). Reason: chunk joins must be sample-exact and each risks a click; seeks, pauses and edits still stop a source with the 10 ms ramp. (Batch 10)
- At 1x the playback clock starts (and re-anchors after a seek) the audio render lead + 20 ms ahead (~70 ms on the dev machine), so the first samples are heard together with the first frame. (Batch 10)
- P6 pinning covers only the playback windows ahead of the playhead (up to 15 frames) and what the preview draws, not every frame within ±1 s. Reason: ±1 s at 60 fps is ~1 GB of 1080x1920 frames, over the 768 MB budget; frames just played stay in the LRU. (Batch 09)
- Insert drops (Mod, §6.8) split the clips crossing the insert frame on every unlocked track and shift the right parts with the rest (Premiere's insert with sync locks on), by the moved block's span. Locked tracks never shift and nothing lands on them, so the only insert refusal ("insert collisions are reported by a toast") is a locked destination or source track, reported by an error toast as in overwrite mode. (Batch 11)
- Mod+click toggles the clip alone (§6.7 "add without linked partner"), so a second Mod+click removes it. Alt on an already-selected clip keeps the selection for Alt+drag; Alt+click without a drag selects only that clip. A click on empty space clears the selection on release (a press there may start a marquee). (Batch 11)
- Overwrite trims and splits partition keyframes by frame with no boundary keyframes (eases stay opaque until Batch 35). (Batch 11)
- During a drag the S key is read by the drag's window key listener (capture phase) and stopped there, never reaching the KeyDispatcher (§3.6). Reason: hold-to-invert snapping (§6.9) must work while Shift, Alt or Mod is held, and Shift+S and Mod+S are other commands (linked selection, save). Key-ups pass on; outside drags S goes through the dispatcher. (Batch 12)
- Q / W (§6.10) use one edit point: among the clips under the playhead on targeted, unlocked tracks, the latest start (Q) or the earliest end (W). The clips with their edge there and their linked partners are trimmed to the playhead, and the ripple shifts every unlocked track (the §6.10 ripple rule), limited by collisions. Clips spanning the edit point (a music bed) are left alone; clips after them on their track can limit the ripple. Clips that start at the playhead are not trimmed (W would delete them). Reason: one edit point keeps all tracks in sync, and every track is targeted until the Batch 16 toggles, so trimming each targeted track's own clip would cut a music bed's head. After Q the playhead moves to the edit point, where the frame it showed now is, so playback continues on the same picture (Premiere); W leaves it. (Batch 13)
- Ripple trims: the edit point is the dragged clip's old edge, and on a track holding a trimmed clip (a linked partner) that clip's edge; clips at or after it shift by the change in duration. An in-point ripple keeps the clip's start, so clips on other tracks starting at or after it shift with its content. Locked tracks neither move nor limit: clips never change tracks in a trim, so "collisions with locked clips" (§6.19) cannot occur. (Batch 13)
- The sync badge shows the start offset to the link partner, or the end offset when the starts match (§6.10: an Alt-trim of one side "produces a sync offset badge"; a source-time offset stays 0 after a trim). Batch 16 ("Move into sync") settles the rule. (Batch 13)
- Keyframes follow the source on in trims of video and audio clips only; images, text and solids keep theirs on the clip start (§6.10 names media on and text off; a still has no moving source). Keys out of the new range are kept so a trim back restores them. The "keyframes follow source" setting is Batch 42's settings panel. (Batch 13)
- A trim drag pauses playback, as a ruler scrub does: the preview shows the trimmed edge frame. While the pointer is over the timeline, the view reads Mod on window keydown/keyup only to switch to the ripple cursor; it never handles or stops a key (§3.6). (Batch 13)
- The preview footer's timecode readout is read-only (§7.8: "Timecode readout (editable)"). Reason: the timeline toolbar's timecode field (Batch 07) is the editable playhead field, with the same parser and keyboard jumps; Batch 09's Spec refs take §7.8 for the transport buttons only. Batch 21, which builds the rest of §7.8, makes the readout editable with `bindTimecode`. (Batch 09)

### Batch 06: Video decode engine and frame delivery
- status: done
- date: 2026-10-14
- notes:
  - Protocol (§3.5): `media/workers/protocol.ts` gains a `progress` message (one request can post several frames before its result), the frame priorities `scrub > playback > idle`, and the methods `openVideo`, `requestFrame`, `prefetch`, `closeVideo` and `decodeStats`. `WorkerHost` handlers get an `emit`; a progress value posted after a cancel is closed in the worker. Frame methods are "immediate": they start at once instead of waiting for the two queued slots, because each session orders its own jobs. `WorkerPool.request` takes `onProgress`, and progress that nobody takes is closed.
  - Sample index (§9.2, §9.1): `media/demux/sampleIndex.ts` holds the track in typed arrays: offsets, sizes, dts, cts, durations and sync flags in decode order. Its lookups are display order (by cts), `frameAt`/`positionAt` (binary search), `keyFor` and `feedUntil`. `keyFor` gives the sync sample to start from, one GOP earlier for a leading picture of an open GOP. `feedUntil` is the last decode index the decoder needs to show a frame (B-frames). `sampleIndexFile.ts` stores it in `cache/index/<mediaId>.bin`: magic, version, the source's size and lastModified, codec, description, size and rotation, then the 8-byte-aligned arrays. A mismatched or damaged file reads as null, and the index is rebuilt.
  - `media/decode/VideoDecodeSession.ts` (worker), one per video and lod:
    - Jobs run one at a time, by priority; a prefetch yields to a frame request.
    - A seek feeds the keyframe straight into the configured decoder (see Global deviations). Chunks go in dts order, outputs are matched by cts timestamp, and frames before the target are not delivered.
    - Sequential requests continue without a seek. A frame request feeds only up to `feedUntil` of its target; a prefetch feeds ahead through its range.
    - Backpressure: at most 8 chunks in flight and `decodeQueueSize` below 4.
    - An 8-frame ring serves backward steps, and callers get clones. The end of the track flushes.
    - A decoder that holds frames longer than the content needs (no output for 100 ms while idle) gets one more chunk each time, remembered for the session, and its ring is freed.
    - Leading pictures decoded without their previous GOP are dropped.
    - A decoder that fails before the session ever produced a frame marks the video undecodable (`UnsupportedCodecError`).
    - Scrubs: the keyframe first when the target is 4 or more frames past it, then the newest decoded frame at most every 33 ms across scrubs, once a scrub has waited 16 ms. A fast drag supersedes every request before it completes, so these are what move on screen.
    - `createVideoDecoder` wraps WebCodecs behind `DecoderLike`; `testDecoder.ts` is the test fake.
  - `media/decode/DecodeSessions.ts` (worker registry):
    - `open` loads the cached index or builds it (handing the bytes back for caching) and checks `VideoDecoder.isConfigSupported`. It answers frame times (display order, us), codec, coded size, rotation, `decodable` and `indexFromCache`.
    - `requestFrame` / `prefetch` map times to frames; `close`; stats.
    - A decoder closes after 10 s without requests, and at most 6 are live (LRU among idle ones). The index stays, so a later request only creates a decoder.
    - Wired into `decode.worker.ts`.
  - `media/decode/FrameService.ts` (main thread), `getFrame(mediaId, timeUs, { lod, priority, signal, onApproximate })`:
    - The time snaps to the frame shown at it, and a pool hit answers without the worker. Otherwise the worker's frame is uploaded and closed at once (P5, P13) and put in the `TexturePool` under `(mediaId, lod, frameTimeUs)`. A cached texture is never replaced, because it may be on screen.
    - Stand-ins: the nearest cached frame (new `TexturePool.nearest`) is shown synchronously, inside the call, then any closer frame from the worker.
    - A scrub supersedes the previous scrub of the same media and cancels its prefetches.
    - At most 4 requests per media at the worker; the rest wait, most urgent first.
    - A worker that lost its sessions is reopened once.
    - `prefetch`, `release(mediaId)`, and `attach(source)` per project (file, read and write of `cache/index/`; read-only projects skip the write).
    - Stats: approximate and exact latency (last, p95 of 64), requests, pool hits, superseded, failed, frames received, open frames.
