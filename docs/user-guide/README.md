# Shorts Editor user guide

Shorts Editor is a video editor for short vertical videos (9:16 by default) that runs in your desktop browser. Your projects are folders on your own disk, and your media never leaves your computer. This guide shows you how to set up a project, bring in your media, skim it and add the parts you want to the timeline, arrange, trim, cut and fine-tune it there with thumbnails and waveforms in every clip, mark moments and ranges, organize your tracks, place, resize and rotate the pictures in the frame with snapping and smart guides, type exact values in the Edit Properties panel, crop and reframe pictures, change the speed of clips, hold a frame and punch in at a cut, add styled titles and subtitles with one key and type them right in the preview, make them pop, slide or type themselves in and out, reuse your text styles and animations as presets in one click, caption your video from scratch, from an SRT or VTT file or by transcribing its speech on your own computer, with the spoken word highlighted, check every pixel and the platform safe zones in the preview, and play it back with its sound, smoothly even with 4K footage thanks to proxies.

## What you can do in this version
- Create projects in a folder you choose, with autosave, backups, undo and crash recovery.
- Import video, audio and image files and organize them in the Media panel.
- Skim a clip by pointing at its card, mark just the part you want with <kbd>I</kbd> and <kbd>O</kbd>, and insert or overwrite it at the playhead with a double-click, <kbd>,</kbd> or <kbd>.</kbd>, so a sequence builds up clip after clip.
- Drag media onto any track with snapping, onto the preview to place a picture where you drop it, or drop files from File Explorer straight onto the timeline.
- Move the playhead to any frame.
- Get around the timeline: zoom with the keys, the wheel or the handles of the scrollbar, fill the view with the selection, pan with the **Hand** tool, the middle mouse button or <kbd>Space</kbd>, center the playhead, and read and type times in frames or milliseconds.
- Select clips, drag them to new times and tracks, insert them between other clips, duplicate them and nudge them frame by frame, with every move one undo step.
- Snap clips to the edges of other clips and to the playhead as you drag, and snap the playhead to clip edges in the ruler.
- Trim clips by dragging their edges while the preview shows the new first or last frame, ripple trims that close the gap on every track, and cut to the playhead with Q, W and E, also during playback.
- Roll the cut between two clips, slip a clip's content, slide a clip between its neighbors with a side-by-side preview of both sides of the edit, and change a clip's speed from 0.25x to 4x by stretching it.
- Split clips at the playhead or with the **Razor** tool, delete clips with or without closing the space, close one gap or all gaps at once while the other tracks stay in step, and use the right-click menus of clips and gaps.
- See thumbnails in video clips that get denser as you zoom in, waveforms in audio clips, and badges for speed, sync and links.
- Rename clips and give them one of eight label colors.
- Mark moments with named, colored markers, mark a range with in and out points, play or loop just that range, and remove it from your edit with **Lift** or **Extract** in one step.
- Add, rename, reorder and delete tracks; choose the tracks your edits use; lock tracks to protect them; mute, solo or hide tracks; and make rows taller or shorter.
- Link and unlink a video and its sound, turn **Linked selection** off to edit one side, move clips back into sync, and group clips that belong together.
- Play your edit back frame-accurately, shuttle with J, K and L, loop the whole timeline or a range, and save any frame as a PNG.
- Click a picture in the preview to select it, drag it into place, resize it with its handles (with a snap at 100%), rotate it (in 45° steps with <kbd>Shift</kbd>), nudge it pixel by pixel with the arrow keys, reach pictures underneath with <kbd>Alt</kbd>+click, transform several at once, and bring one forward or send it backward.
- Type or drag exact values in **Edit Properties**: a clip's name, color, start, end and duration, a picture's position, scale, rotation, anchor and opacity, for one clip or many at once. Fit, fill or center pictures with one click, duplicate a clip right after itself with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd>, turn clips off without deleting them, and pick the background color with a color picker.
- Crop pictures in **Edit Properties** or with handles in the preview, and turn a landscape video into a full vertical shot with **Crop to frame** and **Fill frame**.
- Speed clips up or slow them down with one click (0.5x, 1x, 1.5x, 2x) or an exact percentage, with the later clips moving along; hold a frame for 2 seconds with <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd>; and punch in 20% closer at a cut with <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>.
- Snap pictures to the center and edges of the frame, the thirds, the safe areas and each other as you drag, with gold guides and pixel distances; space three pictures evenly; and hold <kbd>Alt</kbd> to measure the distances around a picture.
- Edit 4K and 60 fps phone footage smoothly: the editor makes a light proxy of each heavy video in the background, plays and scrubs on it, and shows the original as soon as you stop. Follow its progress on the media card, and make or remove proxies yourself.
- Zoom the preview up to 3200% at the pointer and pan around, see the real pixels of 4K footage, choose how much detail the preview draws while playing, lay Reels, TikTok or Shorts safe zones, grids and guides over the picture, and fill the window with the preview.
- Hear your edit in sync with the picture, set the listening volume, mute or solo audio tracks, and check your levels on the audio meter.
- Add a title with <kbd>T</kbd> or a subtitle in the lower third with <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>T</kbd> and type it right in the preview, with the caret exactly between the letters at any zoom. Size text with its handles, and style it with eight fonts that come with the editor or the fonts on your computer, a solid or gradient fill, an outline, a shadow and a background box.
- Make titles and subtitles move: choose one of 12 in and out animations, such as **Fade**, **Slide up**, **Word pop**, **Typewriter** or **Line reveal**, watch it play as you click, and set its duration, the delay between words and its easing.
- Save a text style and its animations as a preset and apply them to other titles in one click, seeing each look on your title as you point at the cards. Start from 12 starter presets, put your favorites as chips in the timeline toolbar, choose the presets <kbd>T</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>T</kbd> use, keep presets for all projects or with one project, and share them as JSON files or through a synced folder.
- Caption your video on its own **CC** track: add cues at the playhead or import an SRT or VTT file, fix the text in the **Captions** tab while the preview follows, split and merge cues with one key, give all captions one style and place in **Caption style** or from a text preset, highlight each word as it is spoken (from VTT karaoke files), and export the cues as an SRT or VTT file.
- Transcribe the speech of your edit into captions with one of three Whisper models that run on your own computer, in 99 languages: review the cues before they go in, add them with a highlight on every spoken word, redo just an in/out range, and use it offline once the model is downloaded.

More editing tools (copy and paste), audio editing (clip volume, fades), keyframes, effects and video export arrive in later versions. Their buttons are already visible but dimmed. [What's new](whats-new.md) tracks each addition.

## Conventions in this guide
- **Bold text** is a label exactly as it appears on screen, such as **New project**.
- Keys look like <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>. The guide uses Windows keys; on macOS most <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> shortcuts use <kbd>⌘</kbd> instead, and [Keyboard shortcuts](keyboard-shortcuts.md) lists the exact macOS keys.
- Timecodes read minutes:seconds:frames, so `1:05:10` is 1 minute, 5 seconds and 10 frames. You can switch the editor to minutes:seconds.milliseconds; see [Show times in milliseconds](timeline.md#show-times-in-milliseconds).
- Notes, tips and warnings appear as **Note**, **Tip** and **Important** boxes.
- Each page starts with a **Video placeholder**: the planned walkthrough video for that page, with its file name, length and the steps it will show. The videos are not recorded yet; they will live in the `videos/` folder next to these pages.
