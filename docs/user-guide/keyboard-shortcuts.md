# Keyboard shortcuts

This page lists every keyboard shortcut that works in the current version of Shorts Editor, grouped by area, with its default keys. Use it to play and navigate your edit without the mouse, and to learn which panel a shortcut needs. To change the keys, see [Command palette and custom shortcuts](command-palette-and-custom-shortcuts.md).

## How shortcuts work

**The active panel decides.** The panel you clicked last (or that has the keyboard focus) is the active one: Media, Preview, Timeline or Edit Properties. Clicking the app bar at the top makes no panel active.

- **All panels** shortcuts work wherever you are, also when no panel is active.
- **Panel** shortcuts work only while that panel is active.
- When a panel uses the same key as an all-panels shortcut, the panel wins. When the panel's own action has nothing to act on, or belongs to a later feature, the all-panels shortcut works there. For example, in the Preview panel the arrow keys move the selected layers; with no layer selected there, <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>→</kbd> step frames as everywhere else.

**Typing comes first.** Shortcuts are off while you type in a text field (a name, a timecode field, a number) or edit a text in the preview (there only <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> and <kbd>Esc</kbd> work; see [While you edit text](#while-you-edit-text)), while a dropdown list such as **Sort media by** or **Resolution** has the keyboard focus, and while a dialog or a menu is open. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Escape</kbd> to leave a text field. A dropdown list gives the keyboard back as soon as you pick a value with the mouse; after you pick one with the keyboard, click the preview picture or the timeline. One key works everywhere, also while you type and in dialogs: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd> saves the project.

**Menus take the keyboard.** While a menu is open, the arrow keys, <kbd>Enter</kbd> and <kbd>Space</kbd> work on its rows. When it closes, the keyboard goes back to the panel you were in. See [Menus and lists](#menus-and-lists).

**Your keys.** The tables on this page show the default keys. If you changed keys in **Settings** > **Shortcuts**, yours apply instead, and the tooltips, menus, the command palette and the shortcut sheet (<kbd>?</kbd>) show yours. Many commands that have no key on this page, such as **Normalize**, **Ripple mode** or **Add solid**, can get one there. See [Change a shortcut](command-palette-and-custom-shortcuts.md#change-a-shortcut).

**Icon buttons keep your shortcuts.** Clicking an icon button, such as the transport buttons, **Mute**, **Loop playback** or **Fit the frame in the panel**, choosing from the **Zoom**, **Preview quality** or **Overlays** menu of the preview, or dragging the **Master volume** knob, does not take the keyboard focus, so <kbd>Space</kbd> still plays right after.

**Tooltips show shortcuts.** Hover over a button to see its name and shortcut, the key you use now. Tooltips write the arrow keys as Left, Right, Up and Down.

**macOS.** Where this page says <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>, press <kbd>⌘</kbd> (Command) on a Mac; <kbd>Shift</kbd> is <kbd>⇧</kbd>. On a Mac, tooltips show the symbols, for example ⌘⇧L. On a Mac keyboard without <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> keys, press <kbd>Fn</kbd>+<kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>Fn</kbd>+<kbd>→</kbd>.

**Other keyboard layouts.** Symbol keys such as <kbd>=</kbd>, <kbd>-</kbd> and <kbd>\\</kbd> also work through the key in the same position as on a US keyboard, so they work on layouts where the symbol sits elsewhere.

### Holding keys

- **Arrow keys** (alone, with <kbd>Shift</kbd> or with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>): one step when you press, then, after 0.3 seconds, about 30 steps per second until you release the key. Each step pauses playback first. When the arrow keys move a selected layer in the Preview panel instead, holding them repeats at your keyboard's repeat rate.
- **<kbd>J</kbd> and <kbd>L</kbd>**: holding them down does not repeat or speed up. Only presses count: press again to double the speed.
- **<kbd>K</kbd>**: holding it keeps playback paused and turns <kbd>J</kbd> and <kbd>L</kbd> into one-frame steps.
- **<kbd>Space</kbd> over empty track space or the zoomed preview**: while playback is stopped and the pointer rests on an empty part of a timeline track, or on the preview picture while it is zoomed in (not at **Fit**), holding <kbd>Space</kbd> lets you drag to pan the timeline or the picture. A short press there still plays, but playback starts when you release the key. Over a clip, anywhere else, and during playback, <kbd>Space</kbd> plays and pauses at once. See [Pan the view](timeline.md#pan-the-view) and [Pan the preview](preview-zoom-overlays-fullscreen.md#pan-the-preview).
- If the browser window loses focus while you hold a key, the editor treats the key as released.

- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>0</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>2</kbd> keep the point in the middle of the panel where it is; **Fit** centers the frame. Outside the Preview panel the browser gets these keys; see [Keys the browser keeps](#keys-the-browser-keeps).
- <kbd>Escape</kbd> first cancels a drag in progress (also a layer you move, resize, rotate or crop in the preview) or closes an open menu; the next press finishes a text you are editing (keeping what you typed), then leaves crop mode, then closes the **Remove silence** row, and the press after that leaves fullscreen.
- The arrow keys move layers only while a selected layer is under the playhead. Otherwise <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>→</kbd> (with or without <kbd>Shift</kbd>) step frames in the Preview panel as in the other panels, and <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> do nothing there. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>→</kbd> always step one second.
- Arrow presses less than 0.3 seconds apart are one undo step, **Nudge layer**.
- <kbd>Delete</kbd> also works in the Preview panel: it deletes the selected clips. See [Cutting and deleting](#cutting-and-deleting).

## Keys the browser keeps

In a normal browser tab, the browser keeps some shortcuts for itself, and a web page cannot use them:

| Keys (macOS) | What the browser does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>N</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>N</kbd>) | Opens a new browser window. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>T</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>T</kbd>) | Opens a new tab. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>W</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>W</kbd>) | Closes the tab. |
| <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>←</kbd> (Windows), outside the Timeline panel | Goes back to the previous page in the tab, if there is one. In the Timeline panel the editor keeps it for nudging clips. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>L</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>L</kbd>), outside the Timeline panel | Moves the keyboard to the browser's address bar. In the Timeline panel the editor keeps it for linking clips. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>G</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>G</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>G</kbd>, <kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>⇧</kbd>+<kbd>G</kbd>), outside the Timeline panel | Search the page. In the Timeline panel the editor keeps them for grouping clips. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>0</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>0</kbd>), outside the Preview panel | Resets the browser's page zoom. In the Preview panel the editor keeps it for **Fit**. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>2</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd>, <kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>2</kbd>), outside the Preview panel | Switch to your first or second browser tab. In the Preview panel the editor keeps them for zooming the preview to 100% and 200%. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd>), outside the Timeline and Preview panels | Bookmarks the page. In the Timeline and Preview panels the editor keeps it for **Duplicate**. |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>⇧</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd>), outside the Timeline panel | Bookmarks all open tabs. In the Timeline panel the editor keeps it for the default transition. |

Because of this, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>N</kbd> opens a new browser window instead of a new project. To create a project, press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>N</kbd>, or click **New project** on the start screen.

> **Important:** <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>W</kbd> closes the editor's tab. Your project saves automatically, 1 second after your last change by default (the **Autosave** setting in **Settings** > **General** and in the **Project** section of **Edit Properties**). Before you close the tab, hover over the save dot next to the project name in the app bar and check that it reads "All changes saved".

The browser acts on <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>N</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>T</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>W</kbd> before the editor sees them, also while you record a new key in **Settings** > **Shortcuts**, so you can't give them to a command.
