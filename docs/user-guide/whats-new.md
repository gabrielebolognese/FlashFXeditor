## Batch 50: Version 1.0
- This is Shorts Editor 1.0.0. **Settings** has a third tab, **About**: it shows the version and the renderer (**WebGPU** or **WebGL2**), has **Keyboard shortcuts** and **Command palette** buttons, and **View licenses** opens the list of the open-source packages and fonts that come with the editor. Open it from the command palette with **About Shorts Editor**, also on the start screen. See [See the version and get help](settings.md#see-the-version-and-get-help).
- Each save writes the version of the editor into the project's `project.json`. See [What is in the project folder](projects-and-saving.md#what-is-in-the-project-folder).
- When a trim, a speed change or a frame rate conversion makes a clip shorter than its fade in and fade out together, both now get shorter in proportion and fill the clip, instead of each being cut to the clip's length on its own. See [Know what edits do to fades and transitions](fades-and-transitions.md#know-what-edits-do-to-fades-and-transitions).
- Converting a project to another frame rate now warns before it rounds the media of clips pasted from projects at other rates, instead of converting them without asking. See [When timings are rounded](project-settings-and-templates.md#when-timings-are-rounded).
- WebM files longer than 45 minutes whose sound is decoded whole, such as **Vorbis** sound, are refused at import with the reason, like long .mp3, .aac, .wav and .ogg files. WebM files with **Opus** sound have no limit. See [WebM files and screen recordings](importing-media.md#webm-files-and-screen-recordings) and [Fix import problems](importing-media.md#fix-import-problems).

## Batch 49: WebM files and screen recordings
- Import WebM (.webm) files, such as the screen recordings that Chrome, browser extensions and many recorders save: VP9, AV1, VP8 or H.264 video with Opus or Vorbis sound. They skim, scrub, play, get proxies, thumbnails and waveforms, and export like MP4 files, with the sound in sync. See [WebM files and screen recordings](importing-media.md#webm-files-and-screen-recordings).
- A .webm file with sound only, for example audio downloaded from a video site, imports as an audio item, with no length limit.
- Recordings with uneven gaps between frames keep each frame's time, and show their most common frame rate, for example 30 fps.
- A damaged .webm file is listed under the search field of the **Media** tab with "Not a readable WebM file: it is damaged or is not a video". A WebM file with another video codec, such as HEVC, is refused with a message that names the codec. See [Fix import problems](importing-media.md#fix-import-problems).
- Not supported: .mkv files, and the transparency of WebM overlays (they show opaque). See [A WebM video covers the layers below it](troubleshooting.md#a-webm-video-covers-the-layers-below-it).

## Batch 48: Runs without WebGPU
- The editor now also runs in Chrome and Edge on computers without WebGPU, for example with an older graphics card or a driver the browser blocks for WebGPU. It uses WebGL2 there, by itself, with the same picture in the preview, in **Export frame** and in exported videos. See [Check your browser](getting-started.md#check-your-browser).
- The **This browser cannot run Shorts Editor** screen now asks for **WebGPU (navigator.gpu) or WebGL2**, and lists **A GPU renderer: WebGPU or WebGL2 on a supported GPU with hardware acceleration turned on** when the browser can't use your graphics card. See [No GPU renderer is available](troubleshooting.md#no-gpu-renderer-is-available).
- If WebGPU misbehaves on your computer, add `?renderer=webgl2` to the editor's address to use WebGL2 instead, for the preview and your exports. See [The picture or the export goes wrong only on this computer](troubleshooting.md#the-picture-or-the-export-goes-wrong-only-on-this-computer).
- 4K video played from the original in **Full** quality is smoother with WebGPU than with WebGL2; with the default **Auto** quality, which plays proxies, both play the same. See [Know what to expect from performance](preview-and-playback.md#know-what-to-expect-from-performance).

## Batch 47: A steadier editor
- A file that can't be imported, such as a damaged video or another file renamed to .mp4, now stays listed near the top of the **Media** tab: a red **Failed** badge, the file name and the reason in plain words, for example "Not a readable MP4/MOV file: it is damaged or is not a video". Click the **X** to dismiss it, or run **Dismiss failed imports** from the command palette. See [Check failed imports](importing-media.md#check-failed-imports).
- .mp3, .aac, .wav and .ogg files over 45 minutes are refused at import with that reason, before they can use up the browser's memory. .m4a files and the sound of videos have no limit. See [Supported file types](importing-media.md#supported-file-types).
- HDR videos, such as iPhone videos recorded with **HDR Video** on, no longer look washed out: the preview, paused and while playing, the proxies and exported videos show them with the contrast and colors of a normal screen. See [HDR videos](importing-media.md#hdr-videos).
- If a panel runs into a problem, only that panel stops: it shows, for example, **Timeline stopped working** with a **Reload panel** button, and the other panels keep working. **Reload panel** is also in the command palette. A problem outside the panels shows **Shorts Editor stopped working** with **Reload**, which saves your project first, instead of a blank page. See [Reload a panel that stopped working](workspace.md#reload-a-panel-that-stopped-working).
- If the browser's video decoder crashes while you play, the picture holds for a moment and playback goes on by itself, usually within about 1.5 seconds. See [Know what to expect from performance](preview-and-playback.md#know-what-to-expect-from-performance).
- A video that Chrome refuses partway through playback shows its **Media can't be played** slate right away. In an export, it shows the same slate from that frame on, and the export goes on instead of failing. If the graphics chip is reset during an export, the export stops with **The GPU was reset during the export (device lost). Export again.**, and the next export can run normally. See [Know what goes into the file](exporting-video.md#know-what-goes-into-the-file) and [An export fails](troubleshooting.md#an-export-fails).
- The word count of the **Captions** tab counts the words of Chinese and Japanese text, which is written without spaces, and leaves out punctuation. See [Open the Captions tab](captions.md#open-the-captions-tab).
- A `project.json` that a text editor saved with a byte order mark (BOM) now opens. See [What is in the project folder](projects-and-saving.md#what-is-in-the-project-folder).
- Several rare editing problems are fixed, found by testing thousands of random edits. For example, sound pasted from another project no longer picks a media item of the same name that has no sound, and a volume keyframe moved past the end of its clip no longer lands on the keyframe before it.
- New troubleshooting entries: [A panel shows stopped working](troubleshooting.md#a-panel-shows-stopped-working), [Shorts Editor stopped working fills the window](troubleshooting.md#shorts-editor-stopped-working-fills-the-window), [An HDR video looks less bright than on your phone](troubleshooting.md#an-hdr-video-looks-less-bright-than-on-your-phone) and [The picture stops for a moment during playback](troubleshooting.md#the-picture-stops-for-a-moment-during-playback).

## Batch 45: Faster proxies and a lighter editor
- Long 4K videos get their proxy much sooner. While a video's proxy is being made, the editor makes only the thumbnails you look at (the card picture, the clips on screen and the frames you skim on the card) from the original video; the rest wait for the proxy. On the development laptop, a 2-minute 4K 60 fps HEVC video got its proxy in about 3½ minutes instead of more than 4. See [Follow a proxy while it is made](proxies.md#follow-a-proxy-while-it-is-made) and [Know when thumbnails and waveforms appear](clip-thumbnails-and-waveforms.md#know-when-thumbnails-and-waveforms-appear).
- Closing a project, by opening or creating another one, now frees all the memory it used: its pictures in the graphics card's memory, its decoded sound and video, and its background work. - Right-click a picture for the new layer menu: **Reset transform** (back to fitted and centered), **Bring forward** and **Send backward** (to the next video track up or down, or a new one), **Lock track** and **Delete**. **Copy**, **Paste** and **Duplicate** are there but dimmed until a later version.

- A dropdown list such as **Sort media by** gives the keyboard back to the shortcuts after you pick a value with the mouse.

## Batch 19: Markers, in and out points
- Press <kbd>M</kbd> (or click **Add marker** in the timeline toolbar) to add a marker at the playhead, also while the video plays. Markers are small triangles at the bottom of the ruler; point at one to see its name and time.
- Double-click a marker to name it and give it one of eight colors, drag it to move it (it snaps to clip edges and the playhead), and press <kbd>Delete</kbd> to delete it. Click a marker to edit its label, color and exact time in the new **Marker** section of **Edit Properties**.
- Jump between markers with <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Up</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Down</kbd> (or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>M</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>M</kbd>). <kbd>Up</kbd> and <kbd>Down</kbd> now stop at markers and at the in and out points too.
- Mark a range with <kbd>I</kbd> and <kbd>O</kbd>, or with <kbd>X</kbd> on the clip under the playhead. The range is shaded, and you can drag its brackets in the ruler. <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>I</kbd>, <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>O</kbd> and <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>X</kbd> clear the points; <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>I</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>O</kbd> jump to them.
- **Play in to out** (the brackets button under the preview, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Space</kbd>) plays the range once, or repeats it while **Loop playback** is on. With a range set, **Loop playback** now loops just that range.
- **Lift** (<kbd>;</kbd>) removes the range on the targeted tracks and leaves a gap; **Extract** (<kbd>'</kbd>) removes it and closes it. Each is one undo step.
- Clips, clip edges and the playhead in the ruler now also snap to markers and to the in and out points.
- Right-click the ruler for the new ruler menu: **Add marker**, **Edit marker**, **Delete marker**, **Clear in/out** and **Set in/out to selection**.
- Markers are saved with the project; the in and out points come back when you reopen it.

See [Markers and in/out points](markers-and-in-out-points.md) and [Loop playback](preview-and-playback.md#loop-playback).

## Batch 16: Tracks, links and groups
- Track headers have working toggles: **Target** (crosshair), **Lock** (padlock), **M** (mute), **S** (solo, audio tracks), **Hide** (eye, video tracks) and **Height**. Video tracks can now be muted and hidden: their clips leave the preview.

- Untarget a track to keep it out of <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> with nothing selected, <kbd>Q</kbd>, <kbd>W</kbd>, <kbd>G</kbd> and **Close all gaps**. Double-clicked media goes to the targeted track nearest the divider.

- Tracks come in three heights, S, M and L: click the height toggle, press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Up</kbd> or <kbd>Down</kbd>, or click **Track height** in the toolbar.

- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>G</kbd> groups clips so that one click selects them all; <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>G</kbd> ungroups them. Deleting a grouped clip deletes the group.

## Batch 15: Split, delete and close gaps
- Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd>, or click **Split at playhead**, to split clips at the playhead: the selected clips, or every clip under the playhead when nothing is selected. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> splits every track. Both parts keep everything the clip had, and a video and its sound split together.
- The **Razor** tool (<kbd>C</kbd>) cuts a clip where you click. A white line shows the cut and snaps to clip edges and the playhead. <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click cuts every track at once.
- <kbd>Delete</kbd> removes the selected clips and leaves gaps. <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Delete</kbd> (**Ripple delete**) also closes the space, each track by its own amount.

- When clips move to close a space, the other tracks move with them, so sound and titles stay on their cuts. Where a clip would overlap another, the ripple stops short and a message names the clip in the way.
- Right-click a clip for **Split at playhead**, **Delete**, **Ripple delete**, **Rename** and eight label colors; right-click a gap for **Close gap** and **Close all gaps on track**.

## Batch 14: Roll, slip, slide and rate stretch
- The tools in the timeline toolbar work: **Select** (<kbd>V</kbd>), **Slip** (<kbd>Y</kbd>), **Slide** (<kbd>U</kbd>) and **Rate stretch** (<kbd>R</kbd>). The active tool is gold, and each project remembers it.
- Hold <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Alt</kbd> over the cut between two touching clips and drag to roll the cut: one clip gets longer as the other gets shorter, and nothing else moves. The roll stops when either clip runs out of media, and that side of the cut turns red.
- **Slip** changes which part of its media a clip shows without moving it. **Slide** moves a clip while its neighbors get longer and shorter.
- While you roll, slip or slide, the preview shows two frames side by side, such as the last frame before a cut and the first frame after it.
- **Rate stretch** changes a clip's speed by dragging its start or end, from 0.25x to 4x. The clip keeps the same part of its media, shows a speed badge such as `0.5x`, and its sound plays at the new speed with the pitch changed.
- Click right on a cut to select it as an edit point: red brackets mark it; <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+click selects one side, in gold.
- <kbd>Up</kbd> and <kbd>Down</kbd> jump through every clip start and end on all tracks. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>.</kbd> centers the timeline on the playhead.

## Batch 13: Trimming
- Drag the start or the end of a clip to trim it. The cursor turns into a `[` or `]` bracket over the outer 8 pixels of each end. While you drag, the clip changes live, the tooltip shows how far the edge moved and the clip's new length, and the preview shows the new first or last frame. Releasing makes the trim one undo step.
- Hold <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>, or turn on the new **Ripple mode** toggle in the timeline toolbar, for a ripple trim: the clips after the edit point move with it on every track, so no gap opens.
- A video and its sound trim together. Hold <kbd>Alt</kbd> to trim one side only; linked clips that are out of step then show a red offset such as **-20**.
- Trims stop at 1 frame, at the first and last frame of the media (the edge and **Media start** or **Media end** turn red), at the neighboring clip and at the start of the timeline. The dragged edge snaps like a moved clip.
- Press <kbd>Q</kbd> or <kbd>W</kbd> to ripple trim the start or the end of the clip under the playhead to the playhead, also while the video plays. Press <kbd>E</kbd> to move the nearest edge of the selected clip to the playhead.
